A new benchmark finds that even the best AI research agents fail at tasks human interns handle routinely.

The AARRI-Bench team tested frontier models and agentic systems on 82 manually crafted research tasks. The top configuration, Mini-SWE-Agent with Claude Opus 4.7, achieved only a 68.3% success rate. Agents frequently overlooked subtle yet critical details obvious to human researchers.

The benchmark is narrower than typical AI tests. It focuses on researcher qualities like context awareness, academic integrity, and methodological rigor rather than raw task completion. Tasks are designed to be easy for humans but challenging for agents, revealing gaps in field sensitivity and scientific judgment.

For teams deploying research agents, this suggests current systems require close human supervision on nuanced tasks. The benchmark highlights that reliable autonomous research depends more on replicating human research behavior than on complex scaffolding alone.

Worth reading if you evaluate or deploy AI research assistants. Worth ignoring if you expect agents to replace human judgment entirely.

Paper: Act As a Real Researcher: A Suite of Benchmarks Evaluating Frontier LLMs and Agentic Harnesses in Research Lifecycle, Wang et al.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07462
