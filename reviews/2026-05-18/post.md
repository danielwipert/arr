A team at EPFL has built the first fully open clinical AI model, where every step of its training is documented and inspectable.

Led by Xavier Theimer-Lienhard, the team adapted five open foundation models into medical specialists using a pipeline they call Fully Open Meditron. The process combines eight public medical datasets with clinician-vetted synthetic data, including question-answer pairs grounded in 46,469 clinical practice guidelines. Their best model, Apertus-70B-MeditronFO, improved by 6.6 percentage points over its base on aggregate medical benchmarks, setting a new state of the art among fully open systems.

The finding is narrower than it sounds. The pipeline relies heavily on synthetic data generated by a single teacher model, GPT-OSS-120B, which accounts for roughly 64% of the training examples. While clinician panels vetted the generation prompts, they reviewed only three sampled outputs per template, leaving room for item-level errors. The headline gains should be read as evidence that fully open pipelines can be competitive, not that this specific recipe is a finished product.

For leaders evaluating AI for clinical support, the implication is about vendor risk, not capability. A model whose training data and process are fully disclosed is inherently more auditable than a black-box specialist. This matters for regulatory compliance and for understanding what a model has actually learned. The trade-off is that these open models still lag behind the best proprietary systems, so the choice is between a more inspectable, slightly less capable tool and a more powerful, completely opaque one.

Worth reading if you are responsible for the procurement or risk assessment of clinical AI systems. Worth ignoring if your only metric is benchmark performance.

Paper: Fully Open Meditron: An Auditable Pipeline for Clinical LLMs, Theimer-Lienhard et al.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16215
